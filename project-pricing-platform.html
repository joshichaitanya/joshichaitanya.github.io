Distributed Pricing Platform

A production-style event processing system demonstrating queues, retries, idempotency, caching, observability and AWS-based architecture.

NodeJS TypeScript ReactJS NextJS AWS Lambda Step Functions SQS

Functional Requirements

  • Users or authorized services can upload data files to the system.
  • Validate, normalize, and transform incoming data into a common schema.
  • Persist processed data and historical versions for auditing.
  • Publish data-change events to downstream consumers.
  • Deliver events asynchronously with retry support.
  • Automatically handle failures through retry/reprocessing workflows and notify the responsible teams.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Scalability — support 1M+ records with predictable performance as data volume and processing load increase.
  • Reliability — durable at-least-once event delivery, with idempotent processing and replay/reprocessing capability.
  • Resilience — failure of one downstream consumer must not block ingestion or processing of other consumers.
  • Consistency — follow an eventual-consistency model; downstream consumers may temporarily lag but converge after successful processing.
  • Fault Recovery — automatically retry transient failures and reprocess failed workloads, minimizing manual intervention.
  • Observability & Alerting — provide centralized dashboards, health/status monitoring, and differentiated alarms for system failures and abnormal conditions. Route alerts to the appropriate owning teams for resolution.

Out of Scope

  • Strict ordering of updates across distributed consumers.

Architecture

Prices land via a pre-signed S3 upload or a scheduled third-party API fetch, get enriched and saved to DynamoDB. DynamoDB Streams (CDC) publish each change to an SNS topic, which fans out to per-consumer SQS queues — with a dead-letter queue catching failed retries. On the read path, Redis caches regional pricing enrichment and OpenSearch powers product search.

Architecture Decision Records

Why Step Functions for price-processing orchestration?

  • Multi-step workflow with clear state transitions.
  • Built-in retries/backoff and failure handling.
  • Ability to resume/reprocess from a failed step rather than restarting everything.
  • Makes long-running/asynchronous processing explicit and observable.

Alternative considered: Lambda chaining / SQS-only orchestration.

Trade-off: additional orchestration complexity/cost, but substantially better control over workflow reliability.

Retry/DLQ and failure-recovery strategy?

  • Reprocessing/replay — failed messages land in a dead-letter queue instead of blocking the main flow; once the root cause is fixed, they're replayed from the DLQ to resume processing.
  • Transient vs persistent failures — transient failures (timeouts, brief unavailability) get automatic retries with backoff in place; failures that keep recurring past a retry limit are treated as persistent and routed straight to the DLQ with an alert, rather than retrying forever.
  • Why at-least-once delivery — it reduces the risk of silently dropped updates even if a consumer crashes mid-processing; consumers are built idempotent so the occasional duplicate delivery doesn't cause duplicate side effects.

Alternative considered: exactly-once delivery via a distributed transaction/dedup store.

Trade-off: exactly-once adds significant coordination overhead; at-least-once with idempotent consumers gets the same correctness guarantee more simply.

Why SNS fan-out to per-consumer SQS queues?

  • Decouples producers from consumers — a new downstream team can subscribe without touching the publisher.
  • Each consumer gets its own queue, so a slow or failing consumer can't block delivery to the others.
  • Retries and backpressure are handled per-queue instead of being shared across every downstream system.
  • Keeps the system extensible as more consumers are added.

Alternative considered: direct service-to-service delivery / a shared queue.

Trade-off: an extra pub/sub hop adds a little latency versus direct chaining, but consumer isolation and easy addition of new subscribers are worth it.
