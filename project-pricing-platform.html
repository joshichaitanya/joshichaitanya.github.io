Case study

Distributed Pricing Platform

A production-style event processing system demonstrating queues, retries, idempotency, caching, observability and AWS-based architecture.

Node.js TypeScript AWS SQS DynamoDB

What I did

  • Owned the pricing service end-to-end — a distributed, event-driven platform (S3, SQS, Step Functions, DynamoDB Streams) processing 20K+ daily transactions and 500K international price records with zero data loss.
  • Personally built and maintained 10+ of the platform's 20 microservices — including a product-search microservice on Elasticsearch — provisioned via AWS CDK, working within an 8-10 person squad.
  • Designed and federated GraphQL schemas via Apollo Supergraph across microservices, enabling unified API consumption for downstream teams.
  • Spearheaded an independent cross-team initiative to migrate the pricing service off 3 legacy Oracle-backed provider APIs onto OpenSearch, negotiating schema changes with the owning team to eliminate slow, retry-prone vendor calls.
  • Cut per-price processing time ~30% and failure rate ~5% via the OpenSearch migration, and introduced Redis-based caching for tax/duty calculations — together reducing monthly cloud spend for the pricing platform by ~40%.
  • Established CloudWatch monitoring/alerting standards sustaining 99.9% uptime; enforced SonarQube quality gates, incorporated AI-assisted development (Claude Code, Cursor) into the review workflow, and maintained 80% Jest test coverage.
  • Identified rising security debt at a retro and proposed a weekly Dependabot remediation cadence; the practice was adopted org-wide, reducing per-service vulnerability count to <5%.
  • Assumed full technical ownership of the project for 2–3 months during the team lead's unplanned leave, then independently sustained delivery through a cost-reduction restructuring.
  • Mentored 3 engineers through onboarding, pairing, and monthly 1:1 career guidance.

Architecture

Event-driven pipeline: price events land in S3, fan out via SQS, get orchestrated through Step Functions, and settle into DynamoDB with Streams triggering downstream consumers. Elasticsearch backs product search; Redis caches tax/duty lookups.
